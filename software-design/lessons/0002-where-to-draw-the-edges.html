Where to draw the edges

Recap

In Lesson 1 you saw that a deep module has a small interface and hides a lot. Now the question is: what goes inside one module, and what goes in another?

The key idea: information hiding

Each module owns certain knowledge, or design decisions. The interface should not reveal them. Then you can change the inside without breaking anyone. The opposite is information leakage: other modules depend on the details. (Ousterhout's modular design notes, which credit the Parnas paper.)

The rule of thumb

Bring together when the pieces share information, do one related task, repeat a pattern, or must be handled the same way. You remove dependencies and duplication.

Keep separate when they share little information or truly need different treatment. (Source: the same notes.)

A short way to say it: things that change together belong together. This is my summary of the idea, not Ousterhout's wording.

Try it

1. Two modules both need to know the same data format. What helps most?

2. When is it right to keep things separate?

3. A made-up example: one pricing rule is copied into a web page, an email sender and a report. The rule changes. Best fix?
